# SunCast solar FAQ

## Inverter types
- A grid-tied (on-grid) inverter is the cheapest option. It works together with the grid and switches off during a power cut for safety.
- A hybrid inverter works with the grid and with batteries, so it can supply power during load shedding. It costs more.
- An off-grid inverter works only with batteries and is not connected to the grid.
- The inverter size is usually close to the panel size. For example, a 5 kW panel array normally uses a 5 kW inverter.

## Do I need a battery?
- A battery stores extra solar power for the evening, the night or power cuts.
- It is worth considering if you have long power cuts or use a lot of electricity at night.
- Batteries add a large cost and wear out over time. Lithium batteries generally last longer than lead-acid batteries but cost more.
- If your power cuts are short, a grid-tied system without a battery is usually the cheapest way to save money.

## Net metering and net billing in Pakistan (2026)
- In February 2026 NEPRA replaced net metering with net billing under the NEPRA (Prosumer) Regulations 2026 for new applicants.
- Under net billing, electricity you take from the grid is billed at the normal tariff, and surplus solar power you send to the grid is bought at a much lower buyback rate. Reports give the new rate as roughly Rs 8 to Rs 11 per unit, compared to about Rs 25 to Rs 27 before.
- New contracts are reported to be for 5 years instead of 7.
- Agreements signed before 9 February 2026 are reported to keep their old terms until they expire.
- Rules and rates have changed several times in 2026, so always confirm the current rate and your case with your electricity company (DISCO) or NEPRA before you sign.

## How much can I save?
- Solar saves the most when you use the power yourself during the day, for example for air conditioners, fans, water pumps and washing machines.
- The SunCast estimate is the forecast energy in kWh multiplied by your tariff per unit. It assumes you use all the power yourself. Surplus sent to the grid is paid at a lower rate.
- Real savings depend on your bill slab, your usage and the weather.

## Cleaning and maintenance
- Dust and smog reduce the output of solar panels.
- Clean panels with water and a soft cloth or brush, in the early morning or evening when they are cool. Cold water on hot glass can crack it.
- Clean every few weeks, and more often in dusty seasons.
- Make sure no tree or wall shades the panels. Be careful on the roof and never clean in strong wind.

## Choosing a retailer or installer
- Ask for a written quote that lists the panel brand, inverter brand, warranty, installation time and after-sales support.
- Compare at least three quotes and read recent customer reviews.
- Panels often come with long performance warranties, and inverters usually have shorter warranties.
- Ask who handles the paperwork with your electricity company.
- The retailers shown in SunCast are a starting point. Check them yourself before you pay.

## Safety
- Use an experienced installer with proper earthing and breakers.
- Do not install or repair a solar system yourself. Wrong wiring can cause fires and electric shocks.
