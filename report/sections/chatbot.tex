\section{Chatbot}
Besides the forecast, SunCast has a chatbot that answers common questions
about rooftop solar in Pakistan. It is available in the app and runs in the
backend as the \texttt{/chat} endpoint.

\subsection{Why a Language Model}
People ask solar questions in everyday language, such as whether a battery is
worth it or how net billing works, and the same question can be asked in many
ways, in English, Urdu or Roman Urdu. A rule-based program cannot cover this
(see the AI Justification section), so the chatbot uses a large language
model, the Gemini API.

\subsection{Design}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Knowledge:} a short FAQ file written by the team
        (\texttt{solar\_faq.md}) on inverter types, batteries, net metering
        and net billing, savings, cleaning and choosing a retailer, plus the
        retailers of the user's city from \texttt{data/retailers.csv}. The FAQ
        and the retailers are sent to the model with every question.
  \item \textbf{System prompt:} tells the model to give short and simple
        answers in the user's language, to use only the FAQ and the retailer
        list for facts, and to say so when it is not sure.
  \item \textbf{No made-up facts:} the prompt forbids inventing prices,
        retailers or guaranteed savings, and tells users to confirm current
        rates with their electricity company or NEPRA. This matters because
        the net metering rules changed in 2026, when NEPRA replaced net
        metering with net billing for new applicants.
  \item \textbf{Each message is answered on its own:} the chatbot does not
        keep earlier messages.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Reliability and Security}
The API key is stored as an environment variable on the hosting service and
is never committed to the repository. If the key is missing, or the API fails
or is over its free limit, the chatbot falls back to the FAQ section that best
matches the question, so the app keeps working. Messages are cut to 1000
characters.

\subsection{Limitations}
The answers are only as good as the FAQ, and a language model can still make
mistakes, so the chatbot is a helper and not a replacement for a qualified
installer. The free tier of the API has usage limits, and net billing rates
may change after this report was written.
